How it works

One rubric, applied the same way to every vendor.

We measure two jobs separately: a Shopping Assistant that helps a shopper find and buy, and a Support Agent that resolves shipping, returns, and policy questions without a human. Every agent gets the same questions, under the same conditions, scored the same way.

Same test for all

One standard set of questions, adapted only to each store's catalog.

No easy way out

No question asks for a human, so every handoff is the agent's own choice.

Cold sessions

A fresh browser with no memory on every run, so nothing is warmed up.

Blind scoring

Names hidden, and every claim quoted from the transcript before it counts.

The numbers

Wins and gaps

Where Gorgias wins, and where it does not.

Support Agent

composite #2 of 10 · best answer quality in the field (94)
✓Wins
  • Answer quality 94/100, best in the field. The highest support quality measured under blind judging (field median ~65). Exact policy terms, concrete timeframes, and next steps a customer can act on, not warm boilerplate.
  • Automation 83%, top-tier among vendors that actually answer. Sierra hands over about 37% of support conversations. The agents that contain more resolve almost nothing.
  • Response speed is fine here (~15s). Mid-pack across the field, and faster than our own shopping lane. Support is not where the speed problem lives.
✕Gaps
  • Store-to-store consistency. The best stores score in the high 70s while the weakest sit in the low 40s. The ceiling is top-tier, the floor is uneven, and onboarding and config QA close the gap.
  • Automation only counts when the answers are good. Containing a conversation with a weak answer hides the problem instead of solving it. Rep AI is the cautionary case: high containment, poor answers.

Shopping Assistant

composite #2 of 11 · strong on substance, behind the streaming leaders on response speed
✓Wins
  • Automation 94%, top-tier. Across live Shopping Assistant stores. The only agents that score higher resolve almost nothing. Sierra hands 15% of shopping conversations to a human, DigitalGenius 72%.
  • It recommends real products. Head-to-head comparisons, promo terms cited correctly, consistent purchase nudges, and shopping quality (85) ahead of Envive (80). Most support-first vendors will not recommend at all. Yuma edges higher on a thin 14-conversation, two-store sample, so read that with caution.
  • Zero out-of-channel deflections. It never pushes the shopper to email or phone. Kodif and Siena both do.
✕Gaps
  • Response speed, near the bottom of the field (~18s). Sierra 9.5s, Envive 12.6s. The answer arrives in one block, so the shopper waits at a typing indicator while streaming vendors show substance in a few seconds. The credible bar looks like 10 to 12 seconds.
  • The issue is perceived speed, not substance. The Shopping Assistant renders product cards and prices, and answer quality is ahead of Envive (83 vs 80). The answer just arrives in one block at around 18 seconds, while streaming rivals show substance in a few.
  • Right answer, delivered too slowly. The recommendation and the rich cards are there, they just take too long to appear.
Market learnings

Five things every leader evaluating an AI agent should know.

1
No vendor wins automation, quality, and speed at once.
Every vendor leads on one or two of the three and trails on the third. Gorgias leads on automation and quality, Sierra on response speed. The first to close its gap will define the category, so treat any agent that claims all three today with skepticism.
2
The most common failure is not a wrong answer. It is a wall or a loop.
Authentication walls, repeated order-number demands, clarification loops, and handing everything to a human. Across the field, judges rarely caught made-up facts. They constantly caught agents that would not engage. These are guardrail problems, not model problems, and they are cheap to fix.
3
Configuration matters more than the model.
The same vendor scores well on one store and near zero on another. The result is won in onboarding and quality control, not in the model demo you were shown.
4
Response speed alone is a vanity metric, but slow is still slow.
The fastest agents are rarely the best. Yet the quality leaders still answer in about 10 to 12 seconds, and that is the bar a shopper will wait for.
5
"AI chat" on the box is not always a real agent.
Some widgets are menus of canned replies. Some will not load for anyone but a human. Nearly a third of the widgets we detected produced no real conversation at all. You cannot tell from a demo, which is why a public, repeatable test is worth having.

Method

Real brands, cold sessions, free-typed questions only, no quick-reply shortcuts. Every conversation is captured, then scored blind by an LLM judge using binary, evidence-forced checks. The score is derived from the checks, never picked by the judge.

Quality rubric

Shopping /100: answer 30 · discovery 20 · recommendation 22 · rich elements 18 · close 10. Support /100: resolution 40 · accuracy 25 · actionability 20 · close 15. Spec: runner/eval-rubric.md.

Composite

Weighted per job. Shopping = 40% automation + 35% quality + 25% speed, because response speed drives conversion. Support = 50% automation + 40% quality + 10% speed, because containment is the job. Speed scores 100 at 3 seconds or under, 0 at 22 seconds or over.

Response speed

Mean time to the complete final answer, never stopped on a "let me check…" stall, never on quick-reply chips.

Neutrality

Every live, verified storefront a vendor runs counts, with no vendor-specific exclusions. A former Gorgias-only exclusion rule was removed in July 2026 for exactly this reason.
