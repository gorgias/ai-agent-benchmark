Where Gorgias wins — and where it doesn't

✅ Where Gorgias wins vs. the industry

  • Zero-touch automation — #1 in market. 98% Shopping / 100% Support: the AI carries the conversation end-to-end. Zero human handovers across 26 engaged support conversations; the field ranges 38–93%.
  • It sells. Top-4 shopping quality (62/100): head-to-head product comparisons, promo terms cited correctly, consistent purchase nudges — most "support" vendors refuse to sell at all (DigitalGenius scored 12/100 for refusing every recommendation).
  • It never punts out of channel. 0 deflections ("email us / call us") measured — Kodif deflected 4 support conversations, Siena 3.
  • Consistent coverage. Measurable data on 6 stores, both lanes, every run — the only vendor with a full-fleet baseline you can trend week over week.

❌ Where Gorgias is not winning

  • Speed — 8th of 9. ~18.3s to a full shopping answer vs. Sierra 9.5s / Envive 12.6s. And delivery is atomic: nothing appears until the whole answer lands, while streaming vendors show substance in 1–5s. The credible bar is ~10–12s.
  • Support answer quality — mid-pack. 49/100 vs. Envive/Sierra at 64. Driven by config variance, not the model: one unconfigured store (Baby Bee, business-hours boilerplate, 15/100) cost ~13 points.
  • Rich commerce elements. No product cards on 5 of 6 stores — Envive shows cards + prices + bundles + a "final pick" on every turn; that presentation gap is their entire quality lead (65 vs 62).
  • The composite crown. Envive wins both lanes today. Closing latency alone (~12s) would put Gorgias at #1 in Support and #2 in Shopping at current automation/quality levels.

Five market learnings

  1. Nobody wins automation × quality × speed — the frontier is open. Envive is closest (win on composite, but no order-system depth). Gorgias owns automation, Sierra owns speed+support quality, nobody owns all three. The vendor that closes its gap first owns the category. Ours is speed; theirs is much harder product work.
  2. The #1 failure mode is not wrong answers — it's walls and loops. Auth walls (Ada), order-number demands (Kodif, DG), clarification loops (Sierra), upsell loops (Rep AI), handover-everything (Meta/Grove). Judges rarely caught hallucinations; they constantly caught refusal-to-engage. These are engineering guardrail failures, not model failures — cheap to fix, huge quality upside.
  3. Merchant configuration variance dwarfs vendor model quality. Same vendor, same model: Meta AI scores 66 on quip and 0 on Grove; Siena 84 on Simple Modern and 0 on FIGS; Gorgias 83 on Madura and 15 on Baby Bee. The battle is won in onboarding and config QA, not in the model. This is a product opportunity (see below).
  4. Speed without substance is a vanity metric — but 18s is still too slow. Meta AI is 4x faster than us and 9x worse on quality. Still, the quality leaders (Envive, Sierra support) all answer in 9-13s. ~10-12s to a full final answer appears to be the credible bar; we're at 14-18s.
  5. "AI chat" claims ≠ measurable free-text concierge. Yuma automates tickets behind the scenes (its chat is chip-gated menus); Humind won't load for non-human sessions; 30-40% of widget-detected stores yielded no engageable conversation. Market noise is high — buyers can't tell. An honest, repeated public benchmark is a sales weapon.

What this means for Gorgias — product ideas

  1. Config Health Score + boilerplate detector (ship first). Baby Bee cost us ~13 points of support quality with business-hours boilerplate. Auto-detect "same reply to different questions" and "no policy specifics in answers" in production; alert the merchant and CSM with a fix checklist. Learning #3: config variance is the biggest quality lever in the whole dataset — for us AND against competitors when we're well-configured.
  2. Automation-rate telemetry in-product. Give every merchant the exact metric this benchmark defines: automated / handover / deflected per conversation, trended weekly. Nobody in the market reports "deflected" honestly. It reframes the buying conversation around the metric we win (98-100%).
  3. Rich product cards + in-chat cart actions in Shopping Assistant. Envive's entire quality lead (65 vs 62) is presentation: cards, prices, bundles, computed discounts, a "final pick". Our capability matrix shows no product cards on 5 of 6 Gorgias stores. Closes the only quality gap between us and the composite winner.
  4. Answer-before-authenticate policy. Never require order lookup/auth for generic questions ("what's your return window?"). Ada and Kodif burn entire conversations on this. Learning #2: walls are the top failure mode; guardrail-level fix.
  5. Anti-loop self-check. Agent-side guard: if the last two replies are near-identical and the user's question changed, force a strategy switch or a graceful skip. Rep AI, Kodif and Sierra all shipped this bug to production. One rule eliminates the most embarrassing public failure class.

Engineering ideas

  1. Latency program to ~10s full-answer (and ~3s first token). Sierra proves 9.5s full answers on live stores. Streaming-first UX, parallel retrieval, model tiering on easy turns. We are 8th of 9 on shopping speed; it's 25% of any credible composite.
  2. Continuous competitive benchmark (this system) as infrastructure. Weekly automated runs, per-provider trend lines (already live in the report), regression alerts when a competitor jumps. Nobody else has ground-truth competitor telemetry; it compounds.
  3. LLM-judge evals as a release gate for the AI Agent. Reuse the exact rubrics from this benchmark (relevance/recommendation/rich/close · resolution/accuracy/actionability/close) on golden conversations per release. The judges reliably separated 15-point quality differences invisible in success-rate metrics.

Method & honesty notes

• Cold incognito sessions per conversation; free-typed questions only (quick-reply chips can serve cached replies that fake latency and automation). Latency runs to the true final answer, past "let me check…" stalls. No PII beyond dummy emails. Nothing is fabricated: unanswered turns, dead widgets and handovers are recorded as such.
• Automation rate uses the engaged-conversation denominator (≥1 timed answer or a handover) so early bails count against a vendor; dead/chip-gated widgets are reported separately, not counted for or against.
• Quality = one LLM judge (Claude Sonnet) per conversation on truncated transcripts (last 450 chars/turn); scores are comparative, not absolute. 302 conversations judged; sample sizes per vendor+lane range 6-40 — small cells (Meta AI shopping n=6) are directional.
• Gorgias stores were captured with the same harness, same themes, same rules as competitors. Weakest results (Baby Bee) are shown, not excluded.
