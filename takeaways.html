Market learnings

Five things the whole industry should know.

1
Nobody wins automation × quality × speed — the frontier is open.
Envive is closest (wins on composite, but no order-system depth). Gorgias owns automation, Sierra owns speed + support quality, nobody owns all three. The vendor that closes its gap first owns the category. Ours is speed; theirs is much harder product work.
2
The #1 failure mode is not wrong answers — it's walls and loops.
Auth walls (Ada), order-number demands (Kodif, DG), clarification loops (Sierra), upsell loops (Rep AI), handover-everything (Meta/Grove). Judges rarely caught hallucinations; they constantly caught refusal-to-engage. These are engineering guardrail failures, not model failures — cheap to fix, huge quality upside.
3
Merchant configuration variance dwarfs vendor model quality.
Same vendor, same model: Zendesk scores 66 on quip and 0 on Grove; Siena 84 on Simple Modern and 0 on FIGS. We caught the same in our own set (a non-functional Gorgias store, excluded). The battle is won in onboarding and config QA, not in the model.
4
Speed without substance is a vanity metric — but 18s is still too slow.
Zendesk is 4× faster than us and 9× worse on quality. Still, the quality leaders (Envive, Sierra support) all answer in 9–13s. ~10–12s to a full final answer appears to be the credible bar; we're at 14–18s.
5
"AI chat" claims ≠ measurable free-text concierge.
Yuma automates tickets behind the scenes (its chat is chip-gated menus); Humind won't load for non-human sessions; 30–40% of widget-detected stores yielded no engageable conversation. Market noise is high — buyers can't tell. An honest, repeated public benchmark is a sales weapon.

Method

Real merchants, cold sessions, free-typed questions only — no quick-reply chips. Every conversation captured, then scored blind by an LLM judge via binary, evidence-forced checks — the score is derived from the checks, never picked by the judge.

Quality rubric

Shopping /100: answer 30 · discovery 20 · recommendation 22 · rich elements 18 · close 10. Support /100: resolution 40 · accuracy 25 · actionability 20 · close 15. Spec: runner/eval-rubric.md.

Composite

Weighted per lane. Shopping = 40% automation + 35% quality + 25% speed (latency drives conversion); Support = 50% automation + 40% quality + 10% speed (containment is the job; latency tolerance is higher than in shopping, so speed carries least). Speed scored 100 at ≤3s, 0 at ≥22s. Reweighted 2026-09-03 from 30/20 — see notes/lane-weights-2026-09-03.md.

Latency

Mean time to the complete final answer — never stopped on a "let me check…" stall, never on chips. p75 = 75th-percentile across all timed turns.

Inspection window

You’re narrowing the window to 30 days

A shorter window doesn’t zoom in — it removes evidence. Every vendor loses storefronts, and the ranking starts depending on which stores happened to be captured that fortnight rather than on how good the agents are.

A narrow margin on a thin sample isn’t precision — it’s not having looked yet.

Use the short windows to inspect: spot a recent regression, check whether a fix landed. The ranking stays on 90 days.

Every vendor is scored over the same window, whichever you choose.
