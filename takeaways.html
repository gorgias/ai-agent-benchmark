Head to head

Where Gorgias wins — and where it doesn't.

🛍️ Shopping Assistant — composite #3 of 10 · Envive leads

✅ Wins

  • Automation #1 — 98%. One handover in 40 engaged conversations. Sierra loses 15% of shopping conversations to humans, DigitalGenius 72%.
  • It actually sells — quality now ties Envive (65/100). Head-to-head comparisons, promo terms cited correctly, consistent purchase nudges. Most support-first vendors refuse to recommend at all (DG: 12/100).
  • Zero out-of-channel deflections — never punts the shopper to email/phone (Kodif and Siena both do).

❌ Gaps

  • Speed — 8th of 9 (~18.3s). Sierra 9.5s, Envive 12.6s. Delivery is atomic: the shopper stares at a typing indicator while streaming vendors show substance in 1–5s. The credible bar is 10–12s.
  • Perceived speed, not substance. The V3 Shopping Assistant renders product cards + prices and answer quality now ties Envive (65) — but delivery is atomic, so the shopper waits ~18s at a typing indicator while streaming rivals paint substance in 1–5s.
  • Result: the sale is won on merit, lost on speed. Right answer and rich cards — just delivered too slowly.

🎧 Support — composite #2 of 10 · 70, behind Envive

✅ Wins

  • Automation #1 — 90%. 18 of 20 engaged conversations handled with zero human touch (2 handovers). Sierra hands over ~37% of support conversations; the field ranges 55–90%.
  • On configured stores, resolution quality ties the leaders. Beekman scores 77, Glamnetic 76, Madura 61 — at or above Envive's best (71) and behind only Sierra's Casper (84). The model's ceiling is top-tier.
  • Latency mid-pack, not last — 14.4s vs. field 7–43s, and 4s faster than our own shopping lane.

❌ Gaps

  • Store-to-store consistency, not capability. Beekman scores 77 but Jade/Shoebacca sit ~41-47 — the ceiling is top-tier, the floor is uneven. Onboarding QA (Config Health Score, move #2) closes it.
  • Quality must stay matched to automation. 100% automation only stays a strength if answer quality is uniformly high — Rep AI proves containment alone can hide bad answers.

Market learnings

Five things the whole industry should know.

  1. Nobody wins automation × quality × speed — the frontier is open. Envive is closest (win on composite, but no order-system depth). Gorgias owns automation, Sierra owns speed+support quality, nobody owns all three. The vendor that closes its gap first owns the category. Ours is speed; theirs is much harder product work.
  2. The #1 failure mode is not wrong answers — it's walls and loops. Auth walls (Ada), order-number demands (Kodif, DG), clarification loops (Sierra), upsell loops (Rep AI), handover-everything (Meta/Grove). Judges rarely caught hallucinations; they constantly caught refusal-to-engage. These are engineering guardrail failures, not model failures — cheap to fix, huge quality upside.
  3. Merchant configuration variance dwarfs vendor model quality. Same vendor, same model: Meta AI scores 66 on quip and 0 on Grove; Siena 84 on Simple Modern and 0 on FIGS. We caught the same in our own set (a non-functional Gorgias store, excluded). The battle is won in onboarding and config QA, not in the model. This is a product opportunity (see below).
  4. Speed without substance is a vanity metric — but 18s is still too slow. Meta AI is 4x faster than us and 9x worse on quality. Still, the quality leaders (Envive, Sierra support) all answer in 9-13s. ~10-12s to a full final answer appears to be the credible bar; we're at 14-18s.
  5. "AI chat" claims ≠ measurable free-text concierge. Yuma automates tickets behind the scenes (its chat is chip-gated menus); Humind won't load for non-human sessions; 30-40% of widget-detected stores yielded no engageable conversation. Market noise is high — buyers can't tell. An honest, repeated public benchmark is a sales weapon.
What we do about it

Three moves to #1 — with the math.

Composite = 50% automation + 25% quality + 25% speed. Gorgias today: 71 Shopping (#3) · 70 Support (#2). Envive leads both at 75. Each move below is quantified against that formula, from this benchmark's own data.

1 · Latency program → ~12s full answer

  • Impact: Shopping 71 → 79 (#3 → #1). Support 74 → 78. The single biggest lever in the entire benchmark.
  • Evidence it's feasible: Sierra answers in 9.5s on live stores; Envive in 12.6s; our own support lane already runs 4s faster than shopping.
  • Cheapest first win — perceived latency: we deliver atomically (nothing until everything). Show the product cards / first tokens before the full text: fastest vendors put substance on screen in 1–5s.
  • First step: instrument TTFT vs full-answer in the AI Agent pipeline (this benchmark already measures it externally: our first signal ~13.9s — even the ack is slow).

2 · Config Health Score (ship first — it's free)

  • Impact: Support 70 → ~75 by lifting the weak-config stores (Jade/Shoebacca ~41-47) toward Beekman's 77. Combined with move 1: ~79 — clear #1 in both lanes.
  • Evidence: we found a non-functional Gorgias store in our own set (Baby Bee — business-hours boilerplate, no AI answering) and excluded it; a live Config Health Score would have flagged it for the merchant instead. The pattern is industry-wide: Meta AI scores 66 on quip and 0 on Grove; Siena 84 on Simple Modern, 0 on FIGS. Config variance dwarfs model variance.
  • What it is: auto-detect "same reply to different questions" and "no policy specifics in answers" in production; alert merchant + CSM with a fix checklist. This benchmark's boilerplate detector is a working prototype.
  • First step: run the detector on our fleet, rank merchants by config health, fix the bottom decile.

3 · In-chat cart / transact in Shopping Assistant

  • Impact: turns a talking assistant into a selling one. Answer quality already ties Envive (65) and the V3 SA renders product cards — the remaining shopping lever after speed is completing the purchase in-chat.
  • Evidence: only 2 of 14 vendors have a verified working add-to-cart. Gorgias shows product cards but doesn't yet close the transaction in-chat — the LLM judge's "close attempt" dimension is the last place we trail.
  • Also the automation moat: shipping add-to-cart makes our 98% shopping automation transact, not just talk — the one capability no rival owns at scale.
  • First step: working add-to-cart on 2 flagship merchants; re-measure here the following week.

Also worth keeping alive (smaller, cheap): anti-loop self-check (Rep AI/Kodif/Sierra all shipped identical-reply loops to production) · answer-before-authenticate rule (Ada/Kodif burn whole conversations on auth walls) · automation-rate telemetry in-product (nobody reports "deflected" honestly — it reframes the buying conversation around the metric we win) · this benchmark's LLM-judge rubrics as a release gate for the AI Agent.

Fine print

Method & honesty notes.

• Cold incognito sessions per conversation; free-typed questions only (quick-reply chips can serve cached replies that fake latency and automation). Latency runs to the true final answer, past "let me check…" stalls. No PII beyond dummy emails. Nothing is fabricated: unanswered turns, dead widgets and handovers are recorded as such.
• Automation rate uses the engaged-conversation denominator (≥1 timed answer or a handover) so early bails count against a vendor; dead/chip-gated widgets are reported separately, not counted for or against.
• Quality = one LLM judge (Claude Sonnet) per conversation on truncated transcripts (last 450 chars/turn); scores are comparative, not absolute. 332 LLM-judged conversations; sample sizes per vendor+lane range 6-40 — small cells (Meta AI shopping n=6) are directional.
• Gorgias stores were captured with the same harness, same themes, same rules as competitors. One Gorgias store (Baby Bee) was excluded as a non-functional deployment — it returned business-hours boilerplate to every question with no AI answering, the same class as the dead/chip-gated widgets excluded across all vendors. Every functioning store is shown.
